\begin{abstract}
\noindent
LLM agents in production exhibit a failure mode invisible to
single-axis memory benchmarks: the verbal channel and the
action-emitting channel drift apart, with directives ceasing to
reflect anchors the summaries continue to mention. We call this
\emph{stale coherence} and propose a \emph{dual probe}---paired
verbal and action-grounded recall---on tick-continuous
structured-output agent loops. We instantiate it on a deterministic
colony-simulation testbed where one LLM emits directives across
four channels at three nested cadences. A pilot of three model
families (Claude-Sonnet-4.6, GPT-5-mini, DeepSeek-v4-Flash) at two
horizons yields three observations: the dissociation crosses our
pre-registered $|\Delta| > 0.3$ threshold in four of eighteen cells;
magnitude at the 60-minute horizon orders by tier
($0.018 < 0.146 < 0.283$); and direction reverses with horizon for
non-frontier families. To rule out a single-axis-recall reading,
we run an external LongMemEval-style needle-in-haystack probe on
the same models at matched context lengths: every recovered cell
scores perfectly, including the cells whose substrate verbal
recall collapsed. The dual probe measures a substrate-coupled
failure that single-axis benchmarks miss. We release the substrate
and dual-probe toolkit as open source.
\end{abstract}
