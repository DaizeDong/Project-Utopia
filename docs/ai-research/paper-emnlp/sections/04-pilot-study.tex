\section{Pilot Study}
\label{sec:pilot}

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:pilot:setup}

The pilot runs DeepSeek-v4-Flash via an OpenAI-compatible proxy
across three scenarios (\textsf{temperate\_plains},
\textsf{fortified\_basin}, \textsf{rugged\_highlands}), three fixed
seeds drawn from a pre-registered list, and two horizons (10
sim-minutes and 60 sim-minutes), for eighteen LLM-driven cells.
The pre-registered design also crossed the frontier and mid-tier
closed-weight families (Claude-Sonnet-4.6, GPT-5-mini); the
matrix as executed is the single-family subset that completed
under the per-model API availability we had during the study
window, and the cross-family extension is the immediate follow-on.
A no-LLM control runs the same substrate with a stub adapter
that emits constant policy weights; the control yields
$\Delta \approx 0$ by construction and calibrates the $0.3$
threshold against substrate noise. Channel cadences are scaled by
a uniform $30\times$ multiplier to stay below the proxy's
$15$-request-per-minute global rate cap; the multiplier preserves
the relative cadence ratios that produce the dissociation
surface.

\subsection{H1: Threshold-Crossing Existence}
\label{sec:pilot:h1}

\paragraph{Hypothesis.}
$\exists t \leq 4$\,sim-hours such that $|\Delta(t)| > 0.3$ on at
least one pilot cell. We report two horizons (10\,min and 60\,min)
and treat the hypothesis as satisfied if any cell crosses
threshold.

\paragraph{Results.}
Two of eighteen cells exceed $|\Delta| > 0.3$, both at the
10-minute horizon:
\textsf{fortified\_basin} with one seed reaches $\Delta = +0.34$
(verbal lead), and \textsf{rugged\_highlands} with another seed
reaches $\Delta = -0.31$ (action lead). The remaining sixteen
cells sit below threshold. The hypothesis is satisfied; the
dissociation reaches threshold magnitude on the substrate, and
does so within ten sim-minutes rather than requiring the
four-hour ceiling. Table~\ref{tab:e1-stageb} reports the
per-(scenario, horizon) aggregate;
Figure~\ref{fig:alpha:trajectory} overlays the trajectories.

\paragraph{Threshold sensitivity.}
The pre-registered $|\Delta| > 0.3$ threshold is six times the
no-LLM noise floor ($|\Delta| < 0.05$ in the constant-policy
control), but the threshold itself is a choice and a reviewer can
reasonably ask whether the H1 outcome is robust to other choices.
Across the eighteen cells, $|\Delta|$ values above $0.40$ occur in
zero cells, above $0.30$ in two, above $0.25$ in three, above
$0.20$ in three, and above $0.15$ in four. The 0/2/3/3/4
progression is monotone---there is no sudden jump at the
pre-registered threshold---and the H1 outcome at any threshold in
$[0.2, 0.3]$ would be that the hypothesis is satisfied (one to
three cells in the matrix cross). We report this as a sensitivity
note rather than as an additional test: the pre-registered
threshold remains the headline, and the broader matrix is needed
to characterise the sensitivity profile properly.

\subsection{Observation 1: Scenario Stratification at Short Horizon}
\label{sec:pilot:scenario}

The 10-minute means separate by scenario: $+0.240$ for
\textsf{fortified\_basin}, $+0.060$ for \textsf{temperate\_plains},
and $-0.077$ for \textsf{rugged\_highlands}. Both threshold
crossings come from the two non-neutral scenarios---one verbal-lead
(\textsf{fortified\_basin}) and one action-lead
(\textsf{rugged\_highlands})---with \textsf{temperate\_plains}
clustering near zero. Figure~\ref{fig:alpha:dual} shows the
underlying AFR and AGR contributions: in
\textsf{fortified\_basin} AFR runs roughly $0.20$ above AGR at the
short horizon, while in \textsf{rugged\_highlands} AGR runs above
AFR. We report this ordering as an observation rather than as a
statistical claim; with three seeds per scenario the standard
deviation across seeds is comparable in magnitude to the
between-scenario differences, and the strict ordering should be
read as a pattern to be tested in a broader matrix.

\subsection{Observation 2: Horizon Attenuation}
\label{sec:pilot:horizon}

The magnitude attenuates with horizon. The mean $|\Delta|$ across
all nine 10-minute cells is $0.176$, while the mean across all
nine 60-minute cells is $0.069$---a roughly $2.5\times$ reduction.
Per scenario, the 60-minute means all collapse into a band of
width $0.03$ around zero, while the 10-minute means span $0.32$.
The pattern is consistent with a feedback-loop interpretation: the
substrate's action chain (Section~\ref{sec:method:substrate})
returns updated worker positions and resource counts into each
subsequent prompt payload, and the longer the horizon the more
chances the model has to observe and correct its prior dissociation
within the same continuous loop. We frame this as a directional
observation; teasing apart correction from natural drift requires
finer-grained sampling and the model variants we did not run.

\subsection{H2: Independence from Verbal Recall}
\label{sec:pilot:h2}

\paragraph{Hypothesis.}
A pre-registered corollary asked whether $\Delta$ is independent
of a verbal-only recall proxy. The version of this test we
originally reported used AFR itself as the proxy, but AFR is by
construction a component of $\Delta = \mathrm{AFR} - \mathrm{AGR}$,
so any correlation $\rho(|\Delta|,\mathrm{AFR})$ has a mechanical
floor. We replace the self-referential proxy with an
\emph{external} verbal-recall probe definitionally independent of
every substrate quantity.

\paragraph{External probe design.}
The external probe is a standalone LongMemEval-style
needle-in-haystack benchmark, run on the three pre-registered
model families at matched context lengths. Per cell we construct a
synthetic prompt by injecting the five pilot anchor strings at the
start of a context, filling the middle with seed-deterministic
distractor paragraphs (substrate-tick log-style ``you observed
event X / your policy weights shifted'' lines), and appending a
free-form recall query. The synthetic context targets
${\sim}4\,000$ tokens for the 10-min condition and
${\sim}25\,000$ tokens for the 60-min condition, matching the
order-of-magnitude context length each pilot cell accumulates.
The probe touches no part of the substrate: it has no AGR, no
$\Delta$, no policy directives, no worker grounding.
Appendix~B details the prompt template and the deterministic
distractor generator.

\paragraph{Results.}
The external probe was run on the three-family pilot we
originally executed; on the deepseek-only single-family matrix we
report above, no matched external probe was re-run. Sixteen of
the eighteen original cells produced real-LLM external-probe
responses; two Claude-Sonnet-4.6 60-min cells failed when the
proxy wallet was exhausted by the ${\sim}25{,}000$-token prompts.
Across the sixteen recovered cells, \emph{every model recalled
every anchor in every cell}: the external recall score is $1.000$
in every joinable observation. Because the external recall column
is constant, Spearman $\rho(|\Delta|,
\mathrm{external\_recall})$ is mathematically undefined; we
report the ceiling outcome rather than a numerical correlation.

\paragraph{Reading.}
At the difficulty level the LongMemEval-style probe sets, the
three pre-registered model families (including the DeepSeek-v4-Flash
family reported in Section~\ref{sec:pilot:h1}) recall every
anchor on a standalone retrieval question at the matched
$\sim$4\,000- and $\sim$25\,000-token context lengths. We read
this as ruling out the simplest alternative explanation---that a
low substrate AFR is just a long-context-recall failure---only at
this probe's difficulty level. We do not interpret the ceiling as
positive evidence of decoupling; that interpretation requires a
harder external probe that induces variance, plus a matched rerun
on the exact eighteen DeepSeek cells reported in
Sections~\ref{sec:pilot:h1}--\ref{sec:pilot:horizon}. Both are
open work.

\subsection{H3: Cross-Substrate Replication}
\label{sec:pilot:h3}

\paragraph{Hypothesis.}
$|\Delta| > 0.3$ also surfaces on at least one cell when the dual
probe is re-instantiated on a substrate that shares neither code
nor domain with the colony simulation. If the dissociation were
purely an artefact of the colony substrate's specific shape, a
correctly-built dual probe on a different substrate should fail
to reproduce it.

\paragraph{Cross-substrate design.}
We construct a minimal customer-support dialogue agent loop with
no code overlap with \texttt{project\_utopia.simulation}. Each
turn issues two independent LLM calls---a verbal-summary call
and a tool-call planner call---sharing session history but
independently sampled, so the verbal and action channels have the
same room to drift the multi-cadence colony substrate provides.
Five anchors of the same $(\textsf{verbal\_token},
\textsf{action\_key}, \textsf{target})$ shape are injected into a
persistent session-context block. AFR scores the verbal-summary
blob exactly as Section~\ref{sec:method:probe} defines; AGR
scores the tool-call JSON's \textsf{action} keys above the same
$\tau = 0.3$ priority threshold. The user-side message stream is
sampled deterministically by seed from one of three scenario
scripts (\textsf{easy} / \textsf{noisy} / \textsf{adversarial}).

\paragraph{Results.}
We run an $18$-cell matrix with the same model (DeepSeek-v4-Flash),
the same three seeds, and two horizons (5 and 15 turns---short
and long analogues of the colony pilot's 10- and 60-sim-minute
horizons). Across the eighteen cells, \textbf{ten cross
$|\Delta| > 0.3$ ($56\%$)}, in both directions (six verbal-lead
with $\Delta$ up to $+0.80$, four action-lead with $\Delta$ down
to $-0.80$). Crossings appear in every scenario, with the
\textsf{adversarial} scenario yielding the highest crossing
rate ($4/6$, $|\bar\Delta_{5t}| = 0.40$ action-lead).
Table~\ref{tab:e1-h3} reports per-(scenario, horizon)
aggregates.

\paragraph{Reading.}
The dual probe surfaces threshold-crossing dissociation in both
directions on the dialogue substrate at a rate ($10/18 = 56\%$)
substantially above the colony substrate ($2/18 = 11\%$). We do
not interpret the magnitude difference as a substrate ranking---a
turn-based dialogue with two independently-sampled LLM calls per
turn exposes more drift opportunities than a multi-cadence
simulation with cross-tick context carry---but the central
kill-argument worry that the colony result might be
colony-specific is the wrong worry: the cross-substrate result
goes in the same direction (bidirectional dissociation, both
threshold-crossing cells and threshold-undershooting cells),
with the same probe definitions, on a substrate written from
scratch in a different domain with a different action surface.
The \textsf{adversarial} pattern in particular---a strong
action-lead mean at the short horizon attenuating to zero at the
long horizon---is the same shape the colony's
\textsf{fortified\_basin} scenario showed in the opposite
direction (verbal-lead attenuating). This is one second
instantiation, not a generality proof, but it converts the
closed-loop concern from ``possibly an artefact'' to ``observed
in two independent substrates''.
