\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

LLM-agent benchmarks measure the wrong layer of the agent stack. The dominant
evaluation regime --- ReAct~\citep{Yao2023ReAct}, Toolformer~\citep{Schick2023Toolformer},
Gorilla~\citep{Patil2023Gorilla}, ToolLLM~\citep{Qin2024ToolLLM},
AgentBench~\citep{Liu2024AgentBench}, GAIA~\citep{Mialon2023GAIA}, MINT~\citep{Wang2024MINT}
--- presents the agent with an \emph{open inventory} of $10^3$--$10^4$ tools and scores
whether it selects and parameterizes the right one. Frontier models now do this
competently. The mode that actually breaks deployments is the opposite regime: the tool
set is \emph{fixed and known a priori}, and the agent must orchestrate multiple parallel
decision channels into that fixed surface coherently.

Consider the canonical production failure: at tick $t$ the environment-director emits a
weather update, the npc-policy emits a worker-allocation directive, and the colony-agent
emits a build plan. Each is schema-valid in isolation; jointly they are stale --- the
build plan references a directive evicted from working memory hours earlier, and no
single-channel validator catches the inter-channel drift. This pattern --- one agent,
several parallel channels at distinct cadences, a fixed schema-validated tool surface
--- is canonical in production systems~\citep{FAIR2022Cicero, AnthropicMultiAgent2025}
and large-scale game agents~\citep{Vinyals2019AlphaStar, Berner2019OpenAIFive}, yet no
existing benchmark isolates it. We call this regime \emph{multi-channel orchestration
of a fixed tool surface}. Existing benchmarks evaluate a narrower slice:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Single-agent tool-use over an open inventory}
(ReAct~\citep{Yao2023ReAct}, Toolformer~\citep{Schick2023Toolformer},
Gorilla~\citep{Patil2023Gorilla}, ToolLLM~\citep{Qin2024ToolLLM},
MINT~\citep{Wang2024MINT}, AgentBench~\citep{Liu2024AgentBench},
GAIA~\citep{Mialon2023GAIA}). One LLM dispatches over an open or learned tool set; the
figure of merit is selection accuracy. No parallel decision channels.
\item \textbf{Flat MARL with primitive actions}
(MeltingPot~\citep{Agapiou2022MeltingPot}, Crafter~\citep{Hafner2022Crafter},
BALROG~\citep{Paglieri2025BALROG}). Policies emit primitive actions over a shared
environment, with no natural-language directive surface, no schema, and no parallel
channels within an agent.
\item \textbf{Multi-agent LLM frameworks}
(ChatDev~\citep{Qian2024ChatDev}, MetaGPT~\citep{Hong2024MetaGPT},
AgentVerse~\citep{Chen2024AgentVerse}). Multiple agents collaborate, but each agent
emits a \emph{single} channel; coherence is enforced by inter-agent dialogue rather
than a per-channel schema contract over a fixed tool surface.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Contributions.}
We introduce \textbf{Project-Utopia}, a benchmark for multi-channel orchestration of a
fixed tool surface, with two contributions:
\begin{description}[leftmargin=*]
\item[C1 --- The Action-Space Contract.] Four parallel LLM decision channels
(\emph{environment-director}, \emph{npc-policy}, \emph{strategic-plan},
\emph{colony-agent}) operate at three nested cadences over a fixed 4-tool surface (path
queries over a versioned grid, Boids group steering, FSM intent application, build
planning). Every directive is filtered through a per-channel pydantic schema and
idempotent numerical guardrails (weights $\in [0, 3]$, TTL $\in [8\,h, 24\,h]$,
allowlist-restricted intent and target vocabularies). This schema-plus-guardrail layer
\emph{is} the agent--tool interface contract --- the same role as ToolLLM's typed
function signatures~\citep{Qin2024ToolLLM} generalized to four parallel channels ---
paired with a three-tier reproducibility contract: (i) bit-identical fallback mode
(hashes verified at 30 / 1\,800 / 7\,200 ticks:
\texttt{be19781c\ldots / f7a099a4\ldots / 43cb6aec\ldots}); (ii) hardware-independent
LLM-on mode via LayerCast~\citep{Yuan2025LayerCast}; (iii) stationary-distribution
production-LLM mode.

\item[C2 --- Methodology.] A four-layer metric stack: per-tick dimension plugins emit
raw signal across 19 score keys; a DimensionNormalizer projects via six transforms into
$[0, 1]$ benefit form; MeltingPot-style sandwich
normalization~\citep{Agapiou2022MeltingPot} computes
$(\text{LLM} - \text{fallback})/(\text{oracle} - \text{fallback})$ on aligned per-seed
paired runs; Bayesian Beta-Binomial scoring~\citep{Bowyer2025DontUseClt} with
PolicyValue Baseline variance reduction~\citep{Brown2019Pluribus} yields the final
estimate. Two pre-registered hypotheses isolate the regime:
\textbf{H1 (orchestration advantage)} --- under matched per-decision token budget, the
4-channel contract achieves $\Delta\text{RAE} \geq 0.10$ over a flat single-channel
baseline emitting per-worker FSM-state preferences directly;
\textbf{H2 (schema validity under coherence stress)} --- across 7\,200-tick runs, the
fraction of guardrail-rejected or behaviorally inconsistent directives remains bounded
by the per-channel schema rejection rate, with no compounding inter-channel violation.
\end{description}

\paragraph{Differentiation from prior tool-use frameworks.}
\textbf{ReAct}~\citep{Yao2023ReAct} and \textbf{Toolformer}~\citep{Schick2023Toolformer} keep a single
channel and emit free-form actions over an open inventory; we split the reasoning--acting loop across
four parallel channels with schema-validated directives over a fixed surface.
\textbf{Gorilla}~\citep{Patil2023Gorilla} and \textbf{ToolLLM}~\citep{Qin2024ToolLLM} score API-call
accuracy across $10^3$--$10^4$ REST endpoints; we score long-horizon outcomes downstream of a fixed
4-tool surface, so the failure mode of interest is \emph{coherence across channels} rather than picking
the right endpoint. \textbf{MINT}~\citep{Wang2024MINT} evaluates multi-turn tool use with user feedback,
but the action surface remains a single channel; we add parallel channels and per-tick grounding with
no human-in-the-loop. \textbf{ChatDev}~\citep{Qian2024ChatDev} and \textbf{X-MAS}~\citep{Ma2025XMAS}
compose \emph{multiple agents} (potentially different LLMs); we study \emph{multiple channels within a
single agent} over one fixed tool surface. \textbf{Voyager}~\citep{Wang2023Voyager} discovers new
skills in Minecraft via self-generated code; we hold the tool surface fixed and measure orchestration
under stale context.
