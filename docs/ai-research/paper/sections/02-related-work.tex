\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related-work}

We organize related work along six axes, mapping each to the
contribution claims (C1--C4) of \S\ref{sec:intro}. A complete prior-art
table for the 75+ benchmarks reviewed appears in
Appendix~\ref{app:prior-art-table}.

\subsection{Single-Agent Tool-Use and Web Benchmarks}

AgentBench~\citep{Liu2024AgentBench} ($N$=8 environments),
GAIA~\citep{Mialon2023GAIA} ($N$=466 tasks),
WebArena~\citep{Zhou2024WebArena} ($N$=812 web tasks),
AgentBoard~\citep{Ma2024AgentBoard} (Progress Rate scoring),
BALROG~\citep{Paglieri2025BALROG} ($N$=6 RL games), and recent
TheAgentCompany~\citep{Xu2025TheAgentCompany},
OdysseyBench~\citep{Wang2025OdysseyBench},
HeroBench~\citep{Anokhin2025HeroBench} all evaluate \emph{single}
agents on \emph{episodic} tasks. We share their commitment to
programmatic state-based oracles (rather than LLM-as-judge); we
differ in dispatching \emph{four} parallel decision channels at
distinct cadences within a single continuous task.

\subsection{LLM-in-Game Benchmarks}

Voyager~\citep{Wang2023Voyager} (Minecraft skill discovery),
Cicero~\citep{FAIR2022Cicero} (Diplomacy negotiation),
Generative Agents~\citep{Park2023GenerativeAgents} (Smallville
believability), SmartPlay~\citep{Wu2024SmartPlay} ($N$=6 games $\times$ 9
capabilities), and MineDojo~\citep{Fan2022MineDojo} ($N$=3\,000+ tasks)
established LLM-in-game evaluation. The most direct concurrent
competitor is \textbf{Orak}~\citep{Krafton2026Orak}, which evaluates
LLMs on 12 video games (including Stardew Valley and StarCraft II)
through MCP plug-and-play. Project-Utopia differs by going
\emph{depth-first} on one deep colony simulation with a 96$\times$72
deterministic tile economy, FSM workers, and a multi-resource
processing chain --- producing per-channel and per-dimension scores
that Orak's flat per-game scalars cannot.

\subsection{Memory and Long-Context Benchmarks}

Long-context evaluation has rapidly matured in 2024--2026:
RULER~\citep{Hsieh2024RULER} (effective context length, 13 task
families), LongMemEval~\citep{Wu2025LongMemEval} (long-term chat
memory), Lost-in-the-Middle~\citep{Liu2024LostInMiddle} (positional
bias U-shape), InfiniteBench~\citep{Zhang2024InfiniteBench}
($\geq$\,100k tokens), and the most recent
MemoryArena~\citep{He2026MemoryArena},
MemoryAgentBench~\citep{Hu2026MemoryAgentBench},
Evo-Memory~\citep{EvoMemory2025},
Memora~\citep{Memora2026}, and HELM Long Context~\citep{Stanford2025HELMLong}.
These probe \emph{verbal} recall on synthetic distractors; only
MemoryArena couples recall to action grounding, but in
\emph{session-discrete} agent loops. Project-Utopia's
contribution is the first three-axis joint analysis
($\text{Recall} \times \text{Drift} \times \text{Performance}$)
on \emph{tick-continuous, naturally-accumulated} context, capturing
a dissociation between verbal-but-not-action recall that prior
single-axis work cannot. The Illusion-of-Diminishing-Returns
analysis~\citep{Sinha2025IllusionDiminishingReturns} provides the
theoretical underpinning for our drift curves.

\subsection{Multi-Agent LLM Coordination}

ChatDev~\citep{Qian2024ChatDev} (5 software-engineering roles
in chat-chain), MetaGPT~\citep{Hong2024MetaGPT} (publish-subscribe
message pool), AgentVerse~\citep{Chen2024AgentVerse} (4-stage
recruitment), CAMEL~\citep{Li2023Camel} (2-agent role-playing), and
Concordia~\citep{Vezhnevets2023Concordia} (Game-Master broker)
established multi-LLM coordination patterns. Crucially,
\textbf{X-MAS}~\citep{Ma2025XMAS} (May 2025) is the first to
\emph{systematically} demonstrate heterogeneous-LLM advantages on
per-task routing across 27 LLMs $\times$ 5 domains $\times$ 5 functions,
and \textbf{Anthropic's Multi-Agent Research
System}~\citep{AnthropicMultiAgent2025} validated intra-family
Opus + Sonnet mixes in production. Subsequent work clarifies that
naive multi-agent debate often \emph{degrades}
performance~\citep{StopOvervaluingMAD2025}, and that
heterogeneity is the antidote.
Project-Utopia's E3 experiment (\S\ref{sec:exp:e3}) extends this
literature by adding three orthogonal axes: \emph{cross-vendor}
mixes (Anthropic + OpenAI + open-weight, vs.\ X-MAS's
mostly single-vendor scoring), \emph{per-channel} routing
(vs.\ per-task), and \emph{long-horizon survival} (vs.\ per-task
isolated runs).

\subsection{MARL and Game-Playing AI Evaluation Methodology}

Although Project-Utopia is not an RL benchmark, our metric stack
draws heavily from RL game-playing AI methodology:
\textbf{Crafter}~\citep{Hafner2022Crafter}'s
geometric-mean composite that punishes single-axis collapse;
\textbf{MeltingPot 2.0}~\citep{Agapiou2022MeltingPot}'s
$(\text{focal} - \text{random}) / (\text{exploiter} - \text{random})$
sandwich normalization; \textbf{Pluribus AIVAT}~\citep{Brown2019Pluribus}'s
variance reduction via PolicyValue Baseline (we report a
2--3$\times$ reduction in Project-Utopia, see \S\ref{sec:exp:e8});
\textbf{OpenAI Five}~\citep{Berner2019OpenAIFive}'s TrueSkill
$(\mu, \sigma)$ for low-sample multi-LLM cells;
\textbf{AlphaStar}~\citep{Vinyals2019AlphaStar}'s league-learning
robustness check; \textbf{OpenSpiel}~\citep{Lanctot2019OpenSpiel}'s
$\alpha$-rank for non-transitive payoff matrices;
\textbf{FTW Capture-the-Flag}~\citep{Jaderberg2019FTW}'s
reaction-time-matched fairness convention.

\subsection{Reproducibility and Methodology}

\citet{Bowyer2025DontUseClt} (ICML 2025 Spotlight) demonstrated
that the central-limit-theorem normal approximation
under-covers at LLM-benchmark sample sizes ($N < 300$) and
recommended Beta-Binomial credible intervals as the community
default; we adopt this throughout.
\citet{Yuan2025LayerCast} (NeurIPS 2025 Oral) showed that bf16 LLM
inference can swing accuracy by 9 percentage points across hardware,
and proposed LayerCast for hardware-independent bit-identical
inference --- enabling our Tier 2 reproducibility claim.
\citet{Cheng2025PeerBench} (NeurIPS 2025 Position Paper) argued
\emph{against} agent-as-judge for high-stakes capability evaluation;
following their guidance, all Project-Utopia metrics are
simulator-objective, never LLM-judged.
\citet{Siddiq2025SoftwareReproducibility} introduced the
Reproducibility Maturity Model (RMM) with seven smell categories;
Project-Utopia targets RMM Tier 4+ (\S\ref{sec:reproducibility}).
\citet{Liu2025BATS} formalized cost-aware Pareto reporting; we adopt
$\alpha \cdot \text{tokens} + \beta \cdot \text{tool\_calls}$
following BATS.
The MathArena~\citep{Stanford2025MathArena} streaming-benchmark
template inspires our scenario rotation (\S\ref{sec:limitations}).
