\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

We report nine experiments across hierarchy, multi-LLM coordination,
multi-resource allocation, long-horizon memory, schema-validated
failure modes, decision-token efficiency, statistical methodology,
and reproducibility. Section~\ref{sec:exp:setup} describes the shared
experimental infrastructure; \S\ref{sec:exp:e1}--\S\ref{sec:exp:e9}
report each experiment.

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:exp:setup}

\textbf{Scenarios.} We use five scenarios spanning two map templates
and three crisis injectors:
\textsc{S-Plains}, \textsc{S-Basin}, \textsc{S-Plains-Famine},
\textsc{S-Basin-Siege}, \textsc{S-Plains-Large}.
\textbf{Seeds.} Main experiments use ten seeds drawn from a fixed list
($\{0\text{xC0FFEE}, \ldots\}$); sensitivity sub-experiments use the
first three.
\textbf{Models.} The model menu spans Anthropic
(Claude-Opus-4-7, Sonnet-4-6, Haiku-4-5),
OpenAI (GPT-5-mini), and open-weight (Hermes-3-7B, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct,
Qwen-2.5-72B-Instruct), plus a no-LLM fallback baseline.
\textbf{Run length.} Default is 7\,200 ticks (240 sim-seconds);
long-horizon E5 uses up to 8 sim-hours.

\subsection{E1 --- Hierarchical Decomposition is Necessary}
\label{sec:exp:e1}

\paragraph{Hypothesis.} Under matched per-decision token budget,
the 4-channel tool-orchestration contract achieves at least
$\Delta \text{RAE} = 0.10$ over a flat single-LLM baseline that
emits per-worker FSM-state preferences directly.

\paragraph{Design.} 2 architectures (\texttt{hierarchical},
\texttt{flat-baseline}) $\times$ 2 scenarios
(\textsc{S-Plains}, \textsc{S-Basin}) $\times$ 10 seeds
$\times$ 1 model (Claude-Sonnet) $=$ 40 runs.
Token budget capped at 200\,k tokens per run; both arms run through
\texttt{Guardrails} so schema validity is matched.

\paragraph{Expected results.} Following the calibration anchor of
ChatDev's $-44\%$ role-removal effect~\citep{Qian2024ChatDev}, we
expect hierarchical RAE $\sim$\,0.65--0.75 vs.\ flat
$\sim$\,0.30--0.55, depending on scenario.

\paragraph{Status.} Pipeline verified end-to-end via
\texttt{npm run bench:paper:E1} (smoke run produced 19
NDJSON rows / 67\,s on FB cell). Full E1 runs pending a one-week
dedicated GPU window. Figure~\ref{fig:e1} previews the bar-chart
layout populated from the fallback-only smoke run.

\subsection{E2 --- Token Cost is Decoupled from World Size}
\label{sec:exp:e2}

\paragraph{Hypothesis.} Pearson correlation between
\texttt{prompt\_tokens / decision} and entity count is
$\rho < 0.20$ --- i.e., the \texttt{pickHighlights}-truncated
PromptPayload effectively decouples token cost from world load.

\paragraph{Design.} World size $\in \{4, 8, 16, 24, 36\}$ initial
workers $\times$ 5 seeds $\times$ 1 scenario (\textsc{S-Plains})
$=$ 25 runs.

\paragraph{Expected result.} Slope of
$\text{tokens/decision} \sim a + b \cdot \text{entityCount}$
with $|b| / a < 0.10$ across all four channels (Figure~\ref{fig:e2}).

\subsection{E3 --- Cross-Vendor Channel-Axis Heterogeneous LLM Mix}
\label{sec:exp:e3}

This is the highest-risk, highest-novelty experiment. Following
X-MAS~\citep{Ma2025XMAS} and Anthropic's Multi-Agent Research
System~\citep{AnthropicMultiAgent2025}, we extend per-task and
intra-family heterogeneity to the previously-unexplored
\emph{cross-vendor channel-axis long-horizon survival} regime.

\paragraph{Hypotheses.}
\textbf{H3a:} Mixed cells (\texttt{XV-DIVERSE-LIGHT})
exceed homogeneous-weak (\texttt{WW}) by at least $\Delta \text{RAE} = 0.15$
(absolute), matching X-MAS's per-domain effect sizes.
\textbf{H3b:} Mixed cells perform within $\Delta \text{RAE} = 0.05$
of homogeneous-strong (\texttt{SS}) at $\sim$\,1/3 the cost
(matching ChatDev's $-44\%$ role-removal as the upper bound on
plausible savings).
\textbf{H3c:} Specialization gradient: \emph{environment-director}
benefits most from a strong model (long-tail decisions);
\emph{npc-policy} benefits least (routine clamps dominate).

\paragraph{Design.} Nine cells (\texttt{FB} fallback,
\texttt{WW}, \texttt{SW}, \texttt{WS}, \texttt{SS},
\texttt{XV-OPUS-SONNET} intra-family,
\texttt{XV-DIVERSE-LIGHT}, \texttt{XV-DIVERSE-STRONG},
\texttt{XV-OPENWEIGHT-ONLY}) $\times$ 2 scenarios $\times$ 10 seeds
$=$ 180 runs. Each cell's per-channel routing is configured in
\texttt{server/config/agent-routing.js}.

\paragraph{Expected results.} Table~\ref{tab:e3-cells} summarizes
the predicted cell ranking by sandwich-normalized RAE composite
and DTE-per-completion-token.

\paragraph{Threats to validity.} (a) prompt leakage --- the strong
model's environment-director output enters the prompt context for
the weak npc-policy channel; we control via a \texttt{SW-blind}
ablation that strips the env-summary from the policy prompt.
(b) Cross-vendor adapter compatibility --- Anthropic and Llama
APIs do not natively speak OpenAI Chat Completions; we route all
non-OpenAI traffic through OpenRouter / LiteLLM proxies and
verify equivalence on a held-out test prompt set.
(c) Token-counter normalization --- different tokenizers yield
different counts; we re-tokenize all prompts with \texttt{tiktoken}
post-hoc to align across vendors.

\subsection{E4 --- Multi-Resource RAE Gini $\times$ Sufficiency}
\label{sec:exp:e4}

\paragraph{Hypothesis.} At matched RAE-sufficiency, stronger models
produce flatter per-resource Gini (more even allocation) than
weaker models, controlling for total throughput.

\paragraph{Design.} 4 model tiers $\times$ 3 scenarios
$\times$ 10 seeds $=$ 120 runs.
Scatter plot: $x = $ \texttt{rae\_sufficiency}, $y =
$ \texttt{rae\_distribution\_gini}, color $= $ tier.

\paragraph{Expected result.} Strong-tier models cluster in the
upper-left (high sufficiency, low Gini); weak tiers in upper-right
("starve to feed only food"). Mann--Whitney $U$ on Gini between
top and bottom tiers, with binned sufficiency, gives $p < 0.01$.

\subsection{E5 --- Three-Axis Joint Memory Analysis}
\label{sec:exp:e5}

This is the methodologically novel experiment: the first joint
analysis of $\text{Recall}(t) \times \text{Drift}(t) \times
\text{Performance}(t)$ on continuous tick-level naturally-accumulated
context.

\paragraph{Hypotheses.}
\textbf{H5a:} Verbal recall decreases monotonically with sim time.
\textbf{H5b:} Drift becomes pronounced (Jaccard $\geq 0.5$) by
$t = 2$\,h.
\textbf{H5c:} Performance lag $\geq 30$ sim-min after recall starts
declining; the LLM continues to act effectively on current
observations even when memory of past directives has decayed,
\emph{until} the decision involves a non-currently-visible
constraint.
\textbf{H5d:} Lost-in-the-middle U-shape replicates: positioning
anchors in the middle of the prompt context produces $\geq 30\%$
extra recall drop vs.\ head/tail positioning, matching
\citet{Liu2024LostInMiddle}.
\textbf{H5e:} Action-grounded recall decays faster than verbal
recall (the LLM's strategic-summary may still mention the anchor
even after directives have stopped reflecting it).
\textbf{H5f:} Tick-continuous recall decay is at least $2\times$
session-discrete recall decay, matched on absolute sim-time.

\paragraph{Design.} 4 run lengths (30\,min, 2\,h, 8\,h, 24\,h)
$\times$ 3 models $\times$ 5 seeds $=$ 60 runs (24\,h tier reduced
to 3 seeds for $\sim$\,80 GPU-hour budget).
Each run pre-injects 5 anchor observations at $t = 0$ via
\texttt{AnchorInjector} (importance-weighted MemoryStore eviction
ensures anchors survive routine observation floods).
Anchors carry both verbal tokens (e.g., "warehouse at (12,8)")
and \texttt{implicitGoals} (e.g., \{deliver, warehouse\}) for the
action-grounded probe.

\paragraph{Expected curves.} Figure~\ref{fig:e5} shows three
plots over sim time: \texttt{anchored\_fact\_recall},
\texttt{action\_grounded\_recall} (decaying $\sim$\,30\,min ahead
of verbal), and \texttt{behavioral\_drift} (Jaccard saturating as
recall collapses). The fourth axis (\texttt{performance\_at\_t})
and the lost-in-the-middle U-shape ($\text{Recall}$ vs.\ context
position) are figure stubs --- populated when the real-LLM
long-horizon E5 run lands (see ``Lost-in-the-middle'' below).

\paragraph{Lost-in-the-middle (companion plot).}
The companion U-shape figure replicating
\citet{Liu2024LostInMiddle} on $\text{Recall}(\text{position})$
requires controlled anchor-position runs not present in the current
fallback NDJSON. It is reserved for the real-LLM E5 batch; see
hypothesis H5d above.

\paragraph{Differentiation from concurrent work.}
MemoryArena~\citep{He2026MemoryArena} measures action-grounded
recall in session-discrete agent loops; our continuous
tick-level setting captures gradient decay rather than step
boundaries.
MemoryAgentBench~\citep{Hu2026MemoryAgentBench} includes a
Selective Forgetting axis but no environment loop, so it cannot
report a Performance curve.
LongMemEval~\citep{Wu2025LongMemEval} measures verbal recall
only.

\subsection{E6 --- Schema-Validated Failure Mode Profile}
\label{sec:exp:e6}

\paragraph{Hypothesis.} Different LLM families exhibit
\emph{distinct} failure-mode fingerprints (schema reject rate,
timeout rate, parse-fail rate, fallback occupancy) that
correlate with their training data and JSON-mode robustness.
7B-tier open-weight models exhibit at least $5\times$ higher
schema reject rate than Claude-Opus-4.7.

\paragraph{Design.} 7 models $\times$ 2 scenarios $\times$ 5 seeds
$\times$ 1 sim hour $=$ 70 runs. Per-channel schema-rejection
rates are emitted as side-channel telemetry on
\texttt{state.metrics.aiRuntime.schemaErrors}.

\paragraph{Failure-mode matrix.} Reported in Figure~\ref{fig:e6} as
a cell $\times$ failure-mode heatmap (\{schema reject, timeout,
parse fail, fallback occupancy\}); a numeric companion table will
accompany the camera-ready version once the full 7-model sweep lands.

\subsection{E7 --- Decision Token Efficiency}
\label{sec:exp:e7}

\paragraph{Hypothesis.} Spearman rank correlation
$\rho(\text{RAE-rank}, \text{DTE-rank}) < 0.7$, demonstrating
that token efficiency adds ranking information beyond pure
task performance. Specifically, 7B-tier models with frequent
short directives can outrank 70B-tier models with rare rich
directives on the DTE axis.

\paragraph{Design.} Reuses E6 logs for the cross-model comparison;
adds 15 cadence-ablation runs (1 model $\times$ 3 cadence levels
$\times$ 5 seeds) to test whether DTE is robust to the
LLM's call frequency knob.

\subsection{E8 --- Bayesian Beta-Binomial vs.\ Frequentist (post-hoc)}
\label{sec:exp:e8}

Post-hoc analysis on E1--E7 logs.
\textbf{Hypothesis.} Bayesian Beta-Binomial ranking stability at
$N = 5$ seeds is at least 30\% better than frequentist Wald-CI
ranking, measured as Kendall's $\tau$ across bootstrap subsamples.

\paragraph{Status.} No additional runs needed. Figure~\ref{fig:e8}
previews the ranking-stability line chart vs.\ $N$ for the two
methods; the final curves come from bootstrap on E1--E7 NDJSON logs.
