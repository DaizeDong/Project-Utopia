\subsection{Layer 1: Per-Tick Dimension Plugins}

Five plugins (\texttt{project\_utopia/benchmark/dimensions/}) emit a per-cell
score row of 19 keys:

\paragraph{ResourceAllocationEfficiency} ($N$=5):
\texttt{rae\_composite}, \texttt{rae\_sufficiency},
\texttt{rae\_distribution\_gini},
\texttt{rae\_idle\_capacity},
\texttt{rae\_path\_overhead}.
\texttt{rae\_composite} is the Crafter geometric mean
\citep{Hafner2022Crafter} over per-resource sufficiency:
$$
S_{\text{RAE}} = \exp\!\Bigg(\frac{1}{3}\sum_{i \in \{f,w,s\}}
\ln(1 + s_i)\Bigg) - 1, \quad s_i \in [0,1]
$$
where $s_f, s_w, s_s$ are food/wood/stone per-capita
sufficiencies. Geometric mean punishes single-resource starvation:
$s_f = 0$ collapses the composite to 0 even if $s_w = s_s = 1$.

\paragraph{GroupDynamics} ($N$=4): \texttt{intent\_entropy},
\texttt{coalition\_coupling}, \texttt{state\_target\_obedience},
\texttt{faction\_responsiveness}.

\paragraph{MemoryDegradation} ($N$=4):
\texttt{anchored\_fact\_recall} (verbal),
\texttt{action\_grounded\_recall} (does the directive distribution
still reflect the anchor's implicit goal),
\texttt{behavioral\_drift} (Jaccard distance of action-token sets
between now and $t = 0$),
\texttt{performance\_at\_t} (task signal).
These four keys are emitted by the plugin and surface in NDJSON
output, but are not used as evidence for either pre-registered
hypothesis under the current narrow scope; multi-hour memory
analyses are deferred (see \S\ref{sec:limitations}).

\paragraph{DecisionTokenEfficiency} ($N$=3):
\texttt{dte\_per\_completion\_token},
\texttt{dte\_per\_decision},
\texttt{first\_token\_latency\_p50}.
Following the BATS cost-aware Pareto convention~\citep{Liu2025BATS}.

\paragraph{HierarchicalCoordination} ($N$=3):
\texttt{plan\_policy\_alignment},
\texttt{env\_threat\_responsiveness},
\texttt{colony\_cadence\_health}.

\subsection{Layer 2: Dimension Normalizer}

Plugin outputs are heterogeneous: some are already $[0,1]$ benefit
form (e.g., \texttt{rae\_sufficiency}), others are inverted (Gini,
idle-capacity, drift), unbounded (cadence-stddev), symmetric
(correlations $\in$ [-1, 1]), or rates with a natural decay scale
(token-throughput, latency).
Our \texttt{DimensionNormalizer} applies one of six transforms per key:

{\small
\begin{verbatim}
identity:       x
invert:         1 - x        (clip to [0,1])
reciprocal:     1 / x        (for x in [1, inf), 1 = best)
clipScale:      x / scale    (then clip to [0,1])
rescaleSymmetric: (x + 1) / 2
exponentialDecay: 1 - exp(-x / scale)  for "fromZero=true" benefit
                  exp(-x / scale)       for cost (lower = better)
\end{verbatim}
}

\noindent
A reflective unit test enforces that every plugin's
\texttt{scoreDimensions} key is registered exactly once in the
normalizer table, blocking silent drift between plugin and pipeline.

\subsection{Layer 3: Sandwich Normalization}

Following MeltingPot 2.0~\citep{Agapiou2022MeltingPot}, each
normalized score is paired with a fallback (no-LLM) and oracle
(scripted hand-tuned policy) reference run on the
\emph{same seed and scenario}, then projected:
$$
S_{\text{sandwich}}(\text{cell}) = \frac{
  S_{\text{LLM}} - S_{\text{fallback}}
}{
  S_{\text{oracle}} - S_{\text{fallback}}
}.
$$
We do not clip the upper bound: $S > 1$ flags a "superhuman"
LLM run that exceeded our hand-tuned oracle ceiling; this is rare
but informative.

The fallback policy is \texttt{Guardrails}'s production-grade
state-adaptive default (with full clamping but no LLM), not a
random baseline. The oracle is \texttt{ScriptedOraclePolicy}
(\texttt{project\_utopia.benchmark.baselines.scripted\_oracle\_policy}),
hand-tuned per scenario such that it is both schema-valid and
guardrail-idempotent, with verified $\geq +20\%$ RAE over
fallback in pre-pilot runs across all six scenarios.

\subsection{Layer 4a: Bayesian Beta-Binomial Per-Dimension}

Following~\citet{Bowyer2025DontUseClt} (ICML 2025 Spotlight), we
report Bayesian credible intervals rather than central-limit-theorem
Wald intervals (which under-cover at $N < 300$). For each (cell,
scenario, dimension) cube cell with $N$ seed runs producing scores
$x_1, \ldots, x_N \in [0,1]$:
$$
\alpha = 2 + \sum x_i, \qquad
\beta = 2 + N - \sum x_i, \qquad
\text{posterior} \sim \text{Beta}(\alpha, \beta).
$$
We report posterior mean, $[\text{p2.5}, \text{p97.5}]$ credible
interval, and median. The Beta(2, 2) prior is mildly informative
toward 0.5; we report sensitivity to alternative priors
(Jeffreys (0.5, 0.5), uniform (1, 1)) in
Appendix~\ref{app:prior-sensitivity}.

\paragraph{Variance reduction.}
Following Pluribus AIVAT~\citep{Brown2019Pluribus}, we additionally
report a PolicyValue Baseline (PVB) corrected score:
$$
S_{\text{PVB}} = S_{\text{realized}} - V_{F}(s_0) +
\sum_t \big[V_{F}(s_t \mid \text{after } F) - V_{F}(s_t \mid \text{after } A)\big]
$$
where $V_F$ is the fallback policy's expected DevIndex contribution.
We measure $\sim$\,2--3$\times$ variance reduction across our pilot
E1 runs, which is equivalent to $\sim$\,3--5$\times$ fewer seeds for
the same statistical power. We name this estimator \emph{PolicyValue
Baseline} rather than AIVAT because our $V_F$ is an EMA proxy of past
values rather than true action-value sampling; we cite AIVAT as
inspiration but do not claim full unbiasedness in the strict CFR
sense.

\paragraph{Bayesian vs.\ frequentist ranking stability (methodology
check).} As a post-hoc methodology check, we bootstrap-sample our
E1+E6 NDJSON logs to compare Kendall's $\tau$ ranking stability
between the Beta-Binomial posterior (above) and a frequentist Wald
interval at small $N$. Our working calibration target is that the
Beta-Binomial method delivers at least 30\% better ranking stability
at $N=5$ seeds, mirroring~\citet{Bowyer2025DontUseClt}'s ICML 2025
result on the under-coverage of Wald intervals at $N < 300$. This is
not an experiment about LLM behavior --- it is a check that our
chosen aggregation method is justified at the seed counts the rest
of the paper uses --- so we report it here alongside the metric
stack rather than as a standalone experiment.

\subsection{Layer 4b: HELM Mean Win Rate Across Dimensions}

For the cross-model leaderboard, we follow HELM's Mean Win Rate
\citep{Liang2023HELM}: for each pair of models $(m, m')$ and each
(scenario, dimension) pair, compute
$W_{m,m',s,d} = \mathbf{1}[S_{m,s,d} > S_{m',s,d}]$. Then
$$
\text{MWR}_m = \frac{1}{|S| \cdot |D|} \cdot \frac{1}{|M|-1}
\sum_{s,d} \sum_{m' \neq m} W_{m,m',s,d}.
$$
MWR side-steps the unit-incompatibility of the 19 dimension keys
(some $[0,1]$, some unbounded latency, some signed correlations)
that direct averaging would corrupt. When the per-cell payoff
matrix shows non-transitivity (rock-paper-scissor cycles), we
additionally report $\alpha$-rank
\citep{Lanctot2019OpenSpiel}.
